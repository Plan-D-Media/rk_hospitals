General & Laparoscopic Surgery in Barrackpore

Open and keyhole (laparoscopic) surgery for common surgical conditions, with in-house diagnostics before surgery and ICU and HDU care after it.

Open & keyhole surgery ICU & HDU for post-operative care In-house diagnostics up to CT

Overview

The surgery department at Ramakrishna Multispeciality Hospital handles the common surgical conditions that bring patients to hospital — gallstones, hernias, appendicitis and problems that need an operation to put right. Where it is appropriate, surgery is performed laparoscopically, through several small cuts rather than one large one.

Surgery is only part of the picture. Pathology, X-ray and CT are available in-house for the assessment before an operation, and the hospital's ICU and HDU are on site for patients who need closer monitoring afterwards. The 24×7 emergency department means surgical problems that arrive at night are not left waiting for morning.

Procedures & services offered

  • Laparoscopic (keyhole) surgery — performed through small cuts, where the condition allows.
  • Open surgery — when that is the better option for the patient.
  • Surgical consultation and assessment — including a second opinion on whether surgery is needed.
  • Pre-operative diagnostics in-house — pathology, X-ray and CT.
  • Post-operative care in ICU and HDU — for patients who need closer monitoring.
  • Surgical assessment via the 24×7 emergency department — for acute problems.

Frequently asked questions

What is laparoscopic surgery?

Laparoscopic surgery, often called keyhole surgery, is performed through several small cuts using a camera and fine instruments, instead of one large incision. Whether it is suitable depends on the condition and on the individual patient — your surgeon will advise you.

Will I be able to have a scan and blood tests before surgery here?

Yes. Pathology, X-ray and CT scanning are available in-house at the hospital in Barrackpore, along with ECG and Echo, so the assessment before an operation can be completed on site.

What happens if I need close monitoring after my operation?

The hospital has an ICU and an HDU on the same campus. Patients who need closer observation after surgery can be looked after there rather than being transferred to another hospital.

Can surgical emergencies be seen at night?

The hospital's emergency department is open 24×7 with triage, so a patient with an acute surgical problem can be assessed at any hour and admitted if needed.

Is treatment in this department available cashless?

Eligible patients can be treated cashless under government health schemes, PSU empanelments and the major TPAs the hospital is empanelled with. Please carry your scheme or insurance details so the desk can verify eligibility before admission.
