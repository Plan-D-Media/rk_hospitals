Critical Care — ICU, HDU, NICU & PICU

Complete critical care under one roof in Barrackpore — intensive and high-dependency units for adults, and dedicated neonatal and paediatric intensive care for newborns and children.

ICU & HDU for adults NICU & PICU for children 24×7 emergency backup

Overview

Critical care is for patients who are seriously ill or injured and need close, continuous monitoring and support. At Ramakrishna Multispeciality Hospital, critical care runs across four types of unit — for adults, newborns and children — so families in Barrackpore can access intensive care without travelling into central Kolkata.

These units work hand in hand with the 24×7 emergency department and in-house diagnostics up to CT, so a critically ill patient can move quickly from assessment to the right level of care.

Our critical care units

ICU — Intensive Care Unit

The ICU cares for adults who are critically ill and need the highest level of monitoring and support — for example after a major medical emergency, serious infection or complex surgery. Patients here are watched continuously by the critical care team.

HDU — High Dependency Unit

The HDU is a step between the general ward and the ICU. It suits patients who need closer observation and support than a normal ward can give, but who do not need full intensive care — often as they recover and step down from the ICU.

NICU — Neonatal Intensive Care Unit

The NICU cares for newborn babies who need specialised support — such as premature babies or newborns with breathing or feeding difficulties. Tiny patients are looked after in a closely monitored environment designed for neonates.

PICU — Paediatric Intensive Care Unit

The PICU provides intensive care for infants and children who are seriously unwell, with monitoring and treatment suited to younger patients and their families.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ICU and HDU?

The ICU is for the most critically ill patients who need the highest level of monitoring and support. The HDU is a step down — for patients who need closer observation than a general ward, but not full intensive care.

Do you have intensive care for newborns and children?

Yes. The hospital has a NICU for newborn babies and a PICU for infants and children, in addition to the adult ICU and HDU.

Can critical care be admitted through emergency?

Yes. A patient who arrives through the 24×7 emergency department can be moved into the appropriate critical care unit when the team decides it is needed.

Is critical care available cashless?

Eligible patients can be treated cashless under government schemes, PSU empanelments and major TPAs. Please share your scheme or insurance details so the desk can verify eligibility.
